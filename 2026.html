03 / The pipeline

Current capability: start where the cohort becomes measurable.

The defensible funnel begins at Phase I: 10,954 drug–indication programs observed from 2014–2023. The steepest gate is Phase II.

The familiar picture
10,000→250→5→1
Illustration, not probability

Why it misleads

It splices a chosen screening-library count onto an approvals count without tracking one cohort end to end. Screening may involve thousands or millions; that tells you little about the odds of a nominated drug. Treat early discovery as “many → few,” then use observed transition rates from Phase I onward. 05 / The bottlenecks

Capability gaps live between institutions, not inside one ledger.

This is the defense review of the system: where evidence, rights and risk change hands—and where a promising cure can disappear between mandates.

Fund

Governments + philanthropy

Set missions, fund public goods and absorb risk too early or too diffuse for markets.

Anchor: NIH $47.1B, FY2024
Discover

Universities + institutes

Create mechanisms, targets, tools and talent. Outputs move through papers, patents, licenses and people.

Gate: reproducible human relevance
Translate

Biotech + venture

Turn a finding into a product thesis, build a program and carry it through the thin middle.

Gate: investable proof
Develop

Pharma + trial networks

Run expensive sequential experiments, manufacture at scale and assemble the regulatory evidence.

Industry core: ≈$288B, 2024
Deliver

Regulators + payers + care

Decide whether evidence is sufficient, coverage is viable and the treatment can reach the right patient.

$288B ≠ $400B

The sourced $288B is a 2024 commercial estimate of industry pharma R&D. A broader “$400B+ biomedical R&D” frame may also include public agencies, universities, philanthropy, devices, diagnostics and other research—but those ledgers overlap through grants, contracts, licenses and acquisitions. This map therefore shows the $288B industry core plus named public anchors rather than asserting a false all-in total.

07 / Investment priorities

The leverage points move decisive knowledge earlier.

The map suggests a sharper objective than “fund more drugs”: move decisive information earlier, before the largest sequential bets.

01

Human relevance before humans

Animal and cell models are proxies. The first question is whether the target and mechanism are causally relevant in human disease.

Move earlier: human genetics, patient-derived models, tissue-level measurement.
02

Mechanism before efficacy

A negative Phase II result is ambiguous if no one knows whether the drug reached and changed the intended biology.

Move earlier: target-engagement biomarkers, exposure–response evidence, interpretable failure criteria.
03

Who benefits before “does it work?”

Averaging across heterogeneous patients can erase a real effect—or manufacture one that does not travel.

Move earlier: patient stratification, diagnostic readiness, prospective subgroup logic.
04

Delivery before approval

An approved medicine is not a delivered cure. Diagnosis, coverage, supply, clinician workflow and adherence are separate gates.

Move earlier: design the care pathway while the evidence package is still being built.
The operating question

What can we know at the 100-candidate stage that we currently discover only at the $100M decision?
